# Distribute

**Distribution** carries work from its maker to the people it is for, and brings back attention, trust, use, and sometimes money. A thing nobody finds has not shipped, and a thing people find but cannot use or buy has not reached them.

```
PEOPLE ----> CHANNEL ----> ATTENTION ----> VISIT ----> NEXT TOUCH ----> USE OR PAY ----> LEARN
who it is    where they    seen by the     a place     a way to reach   the outcome     what worked,
for, and     already are,  right people    worth       them again,      that counts     tested again
their need   what it                       landing     with consent
             rewards
```

A **funnel** is one path of countable events from attention to the outcome, such as impressions, clicks, signups, and payments. Each rate's numerator is the next rate's denominator: clicks over impressions, then signups over those same clicks. Multiply rates along one path, over the same people and time window. To combine paths that do not overlap, add their outcome counts, not their rates. Some people skip events, such as buying without signing up, so count those paths too. Choosing people and channels and learning are work around the funnel, not rates in it.

## Laws

- Nobody can promise reach. Ranking rules are only partly visible from outside. What you control is the work, the experiments, how fast you learn, where people land, and what you offer them.
- Start from the people and their need, not the channel. A channel is only where those people already are.
- Every step is a choice the person makes. Each one needs a reason in their terms; see `principles-language` for calls to action.
- Consent and honesty are part of the work. People can decline, unsubscribe, and leave; every price, term, incentive, deadline, and limit is true and stated in full.
- Promise only what you can deliver, and plan the recovery when delivery fails: notice, refund, or a fix, before the buyer has to ask.

## Heuristics (useful, not guaranteed)

Each holds often enough to start with. Test it on your own numbers, and drop it where it fails.

| Heuristic | Where it breaks | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Each channel rewards a dominant kind of value (conviction, taste, competence, novelty) | Channels reward several values, and they shift over time | Do your own best posts on this channel share a value the weak ones lack? |
| Many small experiments beat one polished piece | Some work, such as a film or a launch event, only lands whole | Is each experiment cheap enough to run several times? |
| A format that wins again is worth making a series | A repeat can win on novelty alone, then fade | Did it win again with a different subject and audience? |
| Owned reach outlasts rented reach | A list decays if it is never sent anything worth opening | Is the list still opening and acting at a steady rate? |
| A payment or deposit is stronger evidence than likes or signups | Payments can be refunded, gifted, or made by people who never use the thing | Did payers go on to use it, and keep paying? |
| Amplify only what already worked unpaid | Some audiences are only reachable by paying | Is paid reach measured against the same outcome as unpaid? |

## Defaults to replace

The same cut posted everywhere, a link to a slow page that shows nothing above the fold, a page with five asks, a list for "updates", counting likes as demand, rates multiplied across mismatched denominators, a benchmark treated as a forecast, a single post read as a verdict, a reason for a hit invented after the fact, false scarcity and countdowns that reset, hidden fees or renewal terms, and an unsubscribe harder than the signup.

## Steps

1. Name the people and the outcome that counts: use, a return visit, a referral, a payment. Payment is one outcome, not the only one.
2. Build the destination: the work doing its job at first sight, claims you can stand behind, one clear next step, and a way to reach the person again that they chose.
3. Choose channels where those people already are, including direct contact, referrals, communities, and places offline. For each, write the value it seems to reward as a hypothesis.
4. Make once, cut for each channel's format and value.
5. Run each post or offer as an experiment, with `principles-improve`'s loop: a hypothesis first, a baseline, enough tries before judging, a conversion window long enough for slow outcomes, and the result recorded. Log every count with its denominator.
6. Tear down what beat or missed your baseline. Each explanation stays a hypothesis until a comparison changes only the suspected cause, with the audience, timing, and exposure held as close as you can, and the difference holds. A repeat that wins again shows the result is repeatable, not why.
7. Ask for commitment early, in rising steps: attention, a way to reach them again, then a payment for a dated, deliverable offer where payment fits. State the full terms, the refund rule, and the delivery date.
8. Choose the next fix by expected gain for its cost, not only by which rate looks lowest; `principles-improve` has the method.
9. Measure past the sale: did people use it, keep using it, and recommend it? That is the result distribution serves.

## Principles

| Principle | Failure | Check |
|---|---|---|
| People before channel | Posting where it is easy instead of where the people are | Can you name who this is for and where they already spend attention? |
| Destination before attention | Visitors sent to a page that cannot serve them | Does the page show the work doing its job, with one next step, fast on a small screen? |
| Chained denominators | A rate measured on impressions multiplied by a count of views | Is each rate's numerator the next rate's denominator, over the same people and time window? |
| Every path counted | Buyers who skipped a step missing from the math | Are paths that skip a step measured and added, without double counting? |
| Right audience | Many views from people who cannot use the work | Of those who arrived, how many could use or buy it? |
| Specific next touch | A list that never acts because it signed up for nothing in particular | Did each person ask for this exact thing? |
| Evidence before explanation | A hit explained by a story nobody tested | Was the suspected cause changed alone, with audience and timing held close, before the reason was trusted? |
| Own numbers over benchmarks | Plans built on someone else's average | Once your own tries are enough to compare, do your rates replace the published ones? |
| Honest terms | Hidden fees, renewal traps, or fake deadlines | Is every term, incentive, and deadline true, stated up front, and as easy to decline as to accept? |
| Deliver or recover | Pre-orders taken for something that slips, then silence | Is there a stated delivery date, and a plan for notice and refunds if it slips? |
| Outcome past the sale | Counting sales that turn into refunds or disuse | Do buyers use it and keep using it? |

## Where to look

- The people the work is for, and where they already spend attention.
- The page every visit lands on, as a first-time visitor on a small screen.
- The path from attention to the outcome that counts, step by step, with each denominator.
- The posts and offers that beat or missed your baseline, and what changed between them.
- Every promise made to a buyer: the price, terms, date, and what happens if it slips.
