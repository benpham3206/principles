# Motion

In one line: Give each beat one idea, show the setup before the payoff, and cut only when the idea changes.

**Motion** arranges change over time for an observer: film, animation, motion graphics, and kinetic type. Decide what each beat claims, and whether a continuous move or a cut should carry it. Use `principles-visual` for how a frame looks, and for motion as an axis of an interface. Use `principles-sound` for loudness, rooms, and mix.

## Laws of the medium

- Continuity can do the work of cutting. A cut announces a new idea. Hold the room when the idea has not changed.
- Each beat carries one claim. If a shot or card needs a paragraph, split the beat or cut the claim.
- Show the setup before the payoff. A payoff with no setup is decoration. A setup with no return is a list.
- Sound is the main quality tell. Hero actions get designed, layered sound in a shared space, or the picture is deliberately mute and still carries those actions.
- Leave room to swell. A bed already at full scale cannot accent a close. Use `principles-sound` for loudness, peak, and clip rules.
- Invent from reasons, not traces. Reuse a reason that worked: continuity, a word that performs its meaning, a delayed correct action. Do not copy a path, lockup, character, or cut.
- Check the work as stills and as measures. A contact sheet must tell the story. A claim of sync must beat chance.

## Defaults to replace

A hard cut on every phrase, a stock whoosh on every move, a bed already at full scale, several claims on one card, a logo in the first seconds, a copied camera path or type lockup, cuts treated as on the beat because the picture looks rhythmic, temporary sound left in the finish, motion that starts before the subject can be read, every shot the same length, a constant cut rate with no breath.

## Form

| Leaf | Principle | Failure | Check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shot | A shot is one claim made from one vantage. Change vantage when the claim or the relationship changes. | Several claims packed into one unbroken view, or a new angle that adds no information | Name the claim of this shot. Would a different angle say something else? |
| Cut | A cut joins two ideas. Use it when a continuous move would hide the change, not to create energy by itself. | Cuts that chop one idea, or a single idea stretched across many unmotivated joins | Compare the outgoing and incoming frames. What new idea does the join add? |
| Hold | A hold is a claim. Length gives weight. The last hold is often the landing. | Every shot the same length, or a close that cuts away before it lands | List shot lengths. Does the longest hold sit on the idea you want remembered? |
| Card | A typographic card carries one short claim, often one word or one line. | A paragraph on a card, so reading fights the motion | Pause on the card. Can you read the whole claim before it leaves? |
| Frame | The hero reads first in the still. Supporting motion stays quieter. | The eye hunts; two things move as hard as each other | Freeze a frame mid-beat. Can you point to the one thing to look at? |
| Path | A camera or object path explains scale, entry, or cause. Invent a path that serves this brief. | A borrowed path that still looks like another work, or a move with no reason | State the path's job in one sentence. Would a still or a shorter move do it? |

## Material

| Leaf | Principle | Failure | Check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kinetic type | Type is readable at its real size and duration. A word may perform its meaning. Motion must not outrun reading. Use `principles-visual` for type as shape and for contrast. | Letters that smear, flip, or leave before they can be read; a generic scale and fade on every word | Play at real size and speed, then slower. Is every word readable, and does any motion act out the sense? |
| Metaphor | A physical metaphor can explain a system. Choose one and hold it. Change the metaphor when the product changes. | A borrowed metaphor that still belongs to another work, or several metaphors in one film | Name the metaphor. Could this brief use it only by copying another work's pictures? If yes, invent a new one. |
| Space | Hold a home room so the observer can rest. Reset with a full field or a quiet beat when a long film needs a breath. | Every scene a new room, or no place to return | What is the home room? Does the film come back to it? |
| Palette in time | Reserve one accent color for one role, often the stressed word or the brand close. Keep the rest quieter. Use `principles-visual` for color roles. | Two accents shouting, or the brand color used from the first frame so the close costs nothing | List each accent and the one moment it marks. Is any accent doing two jobs or none? |
| Sound layers | A hero action is a short transient, a body, and a tail or room, sitting in one shared space, with variation. If a generic whoosh can replace the cue and nothing changes, the sound is not designed. Use `principles-sound` for timbre, rooms, and mix. | One stock whoosh on every move; dry clicks beside a huge hall; identical repeats | Mute the picture. Can you tell the hero actions apart, and do they share a room? |

## Energy

| Leaf | Principle | Failure | Check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accent | Contrast in motion, color, loudness, or cut density marks the moment that matters. When everything is accented, nothing is. | A flourish on every beat | Which one moment is the peak? What around it is quieter? |
| Density | Cut rate and move rate are energy. A burst is an accent. A constant rate, fast or slow, is a texture, not a shape. | The same pace from start to finish | Plot cut or move rate over time. Where does it rise, fall, or rest? |
| Headroom | Leave space between the loudest peak and the clip point so a close can rise. Peaks at or past the clip point are lost contrast, not energy. Use `principles-sound` for the loudness laws. | A bed already at full scale, so the close cannot swell | How far is the loudest peak below clipping? Can the close still rise? |
| Force | Spacing and easing show mass and force. Use `principles-visual` for easing, weight, and deformation of a single move. Across a phrase, a long hold is heavy and a burst of short shots is light or sharp. | Every move the same speed and spacing, so mass never changes | Slow the phrase to a tenth. Does spacing still show what is heavy and what is quick? |

## Observer

| Leaf | Principle | Failure | Check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Format | Aspect, length, and playback context set the grammar. A short vertical piece and a long product film need different density and breath. | A long-film structure shrunk to a few seconds, or a short-form burst stretched until the rate no longer fits the length | Watch it at the real size, ratio, and length. Does the density match the format? |
| Distance | The first meeting may be a muted, autoplaying, or small frame. The first seconds must read without sound and without a large image. | A film that only works full-screen with sound on, shown first as a silent thumb | Watch the first seconds muted, small, and cropped as the feed will crop them. Is the subject clear? |
| Mute or heard | Decide whether the finished work will be heard. If mute, the picture must carry the idea. If heard, do not ship a mute master as the only master. | Silence where sound is expected, or a heard film whose idea vanishes when muted in a feed | State the intended playback. Does the picture still carry the idea muted? If heard, do the hero actions have designed sound? |
| Reading time | On-screen type stays long enough to be read at the real size, including captions that remain during cuts. Use `principles-language` for the words. | Type that leaves early, or copy that sounds like a slogan pile | Read every line aloud in the time it is on screen. Do you finish? |
| Attention | Motion pulls the eye. Spend that pull on the hero. Use `principles-visual` for motion on frequent interface actions and for reducing it. | Many things moving at once, or motion that starts before the subject is clear | What moves, and what should the observer look at? If you still the supporting motion, does the claim get clearer? |

## Composition

| Leaf | Principle | Failure | Check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero | One subject owns the frame and the move. The observer should be able to point to it in a still. | The eye hunts; competing subjects share the same energy | Freeze mid-beat. What do you see first? Is it the intended hero? |
| One idea per beat | Each shot, card, or camera phrase makes a single claim. | Two claims in one beat, so neither lands | Write the claim of each beat in a few words. If you need a clause with "and", split it. |
| Setup and payoff | Something earlier earns the later moment: an unfinished line, a shown object, a command, or a trap. | A payoff that was never prepared, or a setup that never returns | For each payoff, point to the setup frame. For each setup, point to its return. |
| Acts | A longer film needs acts and a breath: a mid-point dip in density, color, or sound so the last act can rise. | A feature cluster with no rest, or a long film at one pace | Mark act breaks. Is there a breath before the close? |
| Close | End after the idea is proven. Hold the brand or wordmark until then, and often reserve the accent color for that landing. A designed tail lets the last picture settle, unless the brief is a punch. | Logo first; a hard stop on the last action; a shout before the proof | What is on the last two seconds? Was the idea already shown? Does the sound settle or cut on purpose? |

## Time

| Leaf | Principle | Failure | Check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Continuity | A continuous camera, a held location, or acting inside one room can carry a whole piece. Cut when the idea changes, or when a continuous move would hide it. | Cuts inside one idea, or a continuous take that buries a needed change | Could this stretch be one shot? If you already have one shot, does any needed change get lost? |
| Rhythm | Rhythm is a pattern of accents the observer can predict, from holds, moves, cuts, or sound. Vary the pattern on purpose. | A grid with no accent, or variation with no pattern | Tap the accents. Do they fall where you meant, and does the pattern change at a planned point? |
| Anticipation | Show the wind-up before the action: a quiet title, an unfinished line, a pause, or a delayed correct move. Use `principles-visual` for anticipation inside a single animated move. | The action arrives with no preparation, or the wind-up never pays off | For each accent, what frame prepares it? |
| Sync | A cut or hit locked to a sound onset is earned, not assumed. Measure the share of cuts that fall within a short window of an onset, often around 60 ms, and compare that share to the fraction of the timeline those windows cover. Dense onsets make accidental alignment common. Step through cuts against the soundtrack if you have no meter. | Cuts called synced because the picture looks rhythmic, or a lock claimed when the rate matches chance | Report the observed share versus chance. If you claim a lock, is the lift clear? |
| Spacing | Time between poses or cuts is the motion. Close spacing reads as quick or light. Wide spacing reads as heavy or calm. Use `principles-visual` for easing a single move. | Even spacing on every beat, so weight never changes | Step through poses or cuts. Do the gaps match the weight you intend? |

## Proof

| Leaf | Principle | Failure | Check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contact sheet | A grid of stills at even intervals, plus a still at each cut, must tell the story without playback. | A film that only makes sense in motion, or a board that hides a missing beat | Read the contact sheet in order. Can you name each beat's claim and find every setup and payoff? |
| Cut rhythm | Count hard cuts over time. A zero-cut or near-zero film can still have many beats. Report rate and its shape, not rate alone. | Energy judged only by how often it cuts, or beats missed because they happen inside one shot | Plot cuts against time. Where is the density, and which beats have no cut? |
| Sync versus chance | Do not claim a picture-sound lock without the comparison in Sync. | A lock claimed from a dense bed or a type rhythm that is not cut to the onset | Is the claimed lock backed by a lift over chance? |
| Pre-flight | Before locking a board or a render, confirm one idea per beat, a clear hero, a setup that pays off, designed or deliberately mute sound on the hero actions, no copied move, and type that reads in its time. | A failure left for the render to hide | Run the list on the board, then on the render. Which item fails? Fix the board. |
