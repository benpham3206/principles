# Refine

**Refinement** revises an existing result so its choices better serve the intent. It can add, remove, or reshape parts, from the structure to the smallest detail. Keep the qualities that already work, including intentional richness.

Use `principles-craft` to set the quality bar and the medium skills to judge specific choices. Use `principles-improve` for measured gains in cost, delay, throughput, or failures; use `principles-simplify` when less complexity is the goal. These can be part of a refinement.

## Steps

1. Return to the intent, audience, constraints, and definition of done. Keep the current version as a baseline. Name what must survive the revision and the quality gap that matters most.
2. Examine the actual work at the three distances from `principles-verify`. Find the largest gap between the intended result and what happens in use. Name the evidence that would support or reject a revision. For measurable claims, compare actual results with the predicted range and the measurement's uncertainty. Investigate a difference larger than both: check the measurement, the implementation, and the assumptions, then revise the model and its conclusions where the evidence warrants (`principles-ground`).
3. Work at the scale of the gap. A wrong structure needs more than polished details. Return to `principles-design` when the approach no longer serves the need. For a failure, find its cause with `principles-lessons`; for a measured bottleneck, use `principles-improve`.
4. Make a bounded, reversible revision with an expected effect. Change coupled parts together when coherence requires it; separate changes when their effects need to be distinguished. Keep the previous version available for comparison and recovery.
5. Compare before and after under relevant conditions. Use the same task, size, position, playback level, or workload where these affect judgment. Measure quantitative claims. For experiential qualities, use direct comparisons and observation of the people the work serves, with named criteria. Record what improved, what worsened, and what remains uncertain.
6. Keep the revision only when the evidence supports it and the constraints hold. A preference is a judgment to explain, not a measurement to invent. Retain the previous version when a revision loses useful qualities without a justified trade-off. For a failure fixed, add a repeatable check that fails without the fix.
7. Check the whole again with `principles-verify`, including accessibility, reliability, recovery, and upkeep where relevant. Remove obsolete complexity without removing useful distinctions or safeguards. Record the decision and its evidence where the next maker will find them.
8. Stop when the definition of done and the chosen quality bar are met. If time or cost ends the work sooner, state the remaining gap. Reopen a criterion only when the need or evidence changes, and record why.

For a clear, reversible edit, one revision and comparison may be enough. Spend more on alternatives and independent judgment when the stakes or uncertainty warrant it.

## Principles

| Principle | Failure | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Revise toward an intent | Changes follow the last opinion or a fashionable style | What intended effect does this revision improve, for whom? |
| Resolve the whole before the detail | Beautiful parts in a result that misses its purpose | Would perfecting this detail close the most important gap? |
| Compare with the prior version | Familiarity or effort mistaken for progress | Can the claimed gain be seen or measured in a relevant comparison? |
| Preserve what earns its place | Plainness mistaken for quality; useful richness or safeguards removed | Which useful qualities survived, and which losses have an explicit reason? |
| Match evidence to the claim | False numeric precision, or a speed claim supported only by preference | Does the evidence show the property being claimed, with uncertainty stated? |
| Finish against a stable bar | Endless polish, or success redefined to fit the deadline | Are the original criteria met, or are the remaining gaps stated? |

Pairs with: before `principles-design`, `principles-craft`; after `principles-verify`.
